Job Market Paper

Who Earns? Universal Pre-K and the Division of Labor Within Households
Abstract
Universal pre-kindergarten (UPK) can increase maternal employment, but whether these employment gains change how earnings are divided within couples is less understood. This paper examines how UPK affects the division of market work and earnings within married couples. Using a stacked triple-difference design that exploits variation in the timing of UPK expansions across five U.S. jurisdictions and children's age eligibility, I find that UPK increases wives' share of couple earnings by 3.4 percentage points, or 11.3 percent relative to the pre-expansion mean. The increase is accompanied by higher labor force participation among wives and a larger share of couple market work, while husbands' labor force participation remains largely unchanged. I also find larger effects on wives' relative earnings below earnings parity. These findings show that the effects of public childcare extend beyond maternal employment to how married couples divide market work and earnings.

Working Papers

Career Outcomes Under Relaxed Constraints: Does the STEM-OPT Extension Affect Employment and Career Paths of Foreign-Born Doctoral Students?
Abstract
This paper asks whether relaxing employment restrictions changes where highly skilled immigrants work, or only whether they work. Using restricted-access microdata from the Survey of Earned Doctorates, I study the 2008 extension of Optional Practical Training for STEM graduates. The extension raised the probability of holding a definite employment commitment at graduation by 2.2 percentage points. The gain, however, accrued to academic rather than industry positions.
